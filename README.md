# AutoMitigate

*A bandit that auto-tunes quantum error-mitigation settings per circuit, per machine, per hour, using a mirror-circuit reward that needs no ideal answer.*

Built for the Qollab + IonQ Global Quantum Hackathon, Oct 9–11 2026. MIT licensed. This effort is supported by Qollab & IonQ.

Demo video: *(link added before submission)* · Qollab project: *(link added at publish)* · Pre-registered analysis plan: [`docs/analysis_plan.md`](docs/analysis_plan.md)

**In 30 seconds.** Open the Qollab project, press Run: a bandit replays nine months of real ibm_fez calibration drift, one pull per day, and tells you which error-mitigation setting to use for your circuit, with the Qiskit snippet to apply it; then the circuit runs for real on the simulator you selected, raw and with readout mitigation side by side. Result of the study behind it: on this chain the best setting never moved (drift value ≤ 0.01 % on five circuits), ε-greedy finds it at 45–79 % lower regret than switching everything on, and on a worse chain the best setting did move (9 switches). Everything below is reproducible from the committed tables.

## What it does

**The problem.** Quantum computers return slightly wrong answers, and a handful of software tricks clean them up: zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE), Pauli twirling and readout-error mitigation (REM). Each helps on some circuits and hurts on others, each costs extra circuits, and the hardware's error rates drift from day to day, so there is no fixed rule for which combination to switch on.

**The tool.** AutoMitigate treats the choice as a multi-armed bandit: eight arms (every on/off combination of the three tricks), a learner that pulls an arm, scores it, updates its belief and pulls again, and a reward that needs no ideal simulation. The reward is a randomised mirror circuit, a twin that applies the gates and then undoes them with a random Pauli layer in the middle, so the right answer is a known bitstring whatever the circuit computes.

**The study.** On top of the tool we ran the experiment the question deserves: on nine months of real ibm_fez calibration data, replayed day by day through a noise model, does the best setting actually change? Pre-registered before the data existed. On this chain it does not: the drift value is at most 0.01 % on all five circuits, a setting chosen once is as good as tracking, the bandit's job is to find it cheaply, and ε-greedy does so at 45–79 % lower regret than switching everything on. On a worse (median-ranked) chain the best setting did move, nine significant switches, so tracking matters exactly where the hardware is worse.

## The headline result

Full text, regenerated by `scripts/analysis.py results-md` after every seed pass, in [`docs/results.md`](docs/results.md). The pre-registered sentence per circuit (20 seeds, 240 daily snapshots of ibm_fez, Feb 1 to Oct 9 2026):

| circuit | drift value (% of static oracle) | significant best-arm switches | ambiguous-day fraction | static oracle | ε-greedy regret [95 % CI] | everything-on (arm 7) | vs arm 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ghz4 | 0.00 | 0 | 0.17 | ZNE + REM | 1.60 [0.99, 2.74] | 4.46 | +64 % |
| ghz8 | 0.00 | 0 | 0.03 | ZNE + REM | 3.26 [2.01, 6.85] | 11.47 | +72 % |
| grover3 | 0.01 | 2 (3 epochs, median 88 days) | 0.02 | ZNE + REM | 3.08 [1.98, 4.20] | 14.87 | +79 % |
| qaoa6 | 0.00 | 0 | 0.00 | ZNE + REM | 3.19 [2.16, 4.34] | 13.73 | +77 % |
| qft3 | 0.00 | 1 (2 epochs, median 120 days) | 0.34 | ZNE + REM | 2.16 [1.19, 5.08] | 3.96 | +45 % |

Drift value = what a perfect per-day tracker gains over the best fixed setting, as a percentage of that fixed setting's cumulative score. A significant switch is a day where the best arm changes by more than 2·SE over the seeds. Regret is dynamic pseudo-regret against the per-day oracle, one pull per day, replayed 200 times on the stored rewards; hyperparameters chosen on seeds 0–9, reported on seeds 10–19.

Secondary findings (all in `docs/results.md`):

- **Learners.** ε-greedy (0.1) is the best learner on every circuit. The Thompson family (plain, discounted, sliding-window, change-detection) sits between ε-greedy and the everything-on default, UCB1 is worst: with rewards in [0, 1] and arm gaps of a few hundredths, the Bernoulli trick and UCB1's bonus both over-explore. On the two circuits with a significant switch, ε-greedy re-locks on the new best arm within 5–12 pulls.
- **Is the mirror score honest?** Per day, its ranking of the eight arms against the real forward error has a median Spearman correlation of 0.88 (ghz4), 0.93 (grover3) and 0.62 (qft3), and the arm it picks costs at most 8 % of the worst arm's forward error (median; 1 % on ghz4, 0 % on grover3). Top-1 agreement is 46 % / 94 % / 29 %, low on ghz4 and qft3 because the top arms are within noise of each other, which is exactly when the choice does not matter.
- **Sensitivities.** Coherent fraction f = 0.3 leaves the headline unchanged; exponential instead of linear ZNE keeps the drift value at or below 0.16 % on every circuit; the shot budget (600 / 1200 / 2400) is reported as a Pareto figure only, since every arm spends the same shots. The median-ranked chain (instead of the best chain) gives a drift value of 0.37 % with 9 significant switches on ghz4.
- **IonQ-like source.** Same tool on a static depolarising model with a synthetic drift schedule (stable, slow, jump). On the jump schedule the everything-on arm stays best on both sides, so this source shows the cost of exploration when there is nothing to track. Local model vs Qollab's Aria-1 simulator on 8 identical single circuits: mean |ΔP| = 0.027 (`data/ionq/spot_check.json`, job IDs inside).

## How it works

| arm | ZNE | twirl | REM | circuits per pull |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | | | | 1 |
| 1 | | | x | 1 (+2 calibration) |
| 2 | | x | | 8 |
| 3 | | x | x | 8 (+2) |
| 4 | x | | | 3 |
| 5 | x | | x | 3 (+2) |
| 6 | x | x | | 24 |
| 7 | x | x | x | 24 (+2) |

- **Reward (mirror score).** Randomised mirror circuit U · Q · Ũ⁻¹ with a random Pauli layer Q and a Pauli-frame-corrected inverse (Proctor et al., arXiv 2008.11294), so the ideal output is a known bitstring. Score = P(target), averaged over the 8 twirl copies, extrapolated 1x/3x/5x → 0x by a linear fit for ZNE arms (clipped to [0, 1], clips flagged), corrected by tensored assignment-matrix inversion for REM arms. Every arm spends the same 1200 shots per pull (1200 / 400 / 150 / 50 shots per circuit for the 1 / 3 / 8 / 24-circuit shapes).
- **Learners.** ε-greedy, UCB1, Thompson sampling (Bernoulli trick), discounted TS (Raj & Kalyani 2017), sliding-window TS (Trovò et al. 2020), change-detection TS (M-UCB detector, Cao et al. 2019). Always plotted against four fixed policies: nothing, everything on, the best fixed arm in hindsight, the per-day oracle.
- **Two data sources, both Qiskit Aer, density-matrix method.** (1) A per-day noise model rebuilt from real ibm_fez calibration data (T1, T2 relaxation; depolarising sized so the average gate infidelity matches the reported error; asymmetric readout), one fixed linear chain per circuit size chosen once on the first complete day. (2) An IonQ-like static model (depolarising p1 = 5·10⁻⁴, p2 = 6·10⁻³, readout flip 4·10⁻³) with synthetic drift: extra per-cz Pauli rate p, cz over-rotation θ, extra readout flip r, settings A = (0.03, 0.05, 0) and B = (0.01, 0.15, 0) from `data/calib/ab.json`.
- **Pipeline.** `scripts/fetch_calibration.py` → `scripts/sweep.py` (one row per day × circuit × arm × seed) → `scripts/analysis.py` (headline, learners, best-arm, proxy, drivers, sensitivity, pareto, figures, results-md) → `scripts/export_replay.py` (the tables the Qollab demo replays). Diagrams of each step in [`docs/diagrams/`](docs/diagrams/).

## Run it

### On Qollab (what the judges do)

Open the project and press **Run**. The form under Run settings picks the circuit (ghz4, qft3, grover3, ghz8, qaoa6), the source (ibm or ionq), the drift schedule (ionq only: stable, slow, jump) and the learner. The replay uses the committed reward tables (`data/replay/`) and finishes in a few seconds: the regret table, a two-panel figure (regret curves; the arm chosen per day against the per-day best arm) and a recommendation with the Qiskit snippet to apply it. Then, with `live = True` (the default), the chosen circuit is executed for real on the backend you selected, raw and with readout mitigation from two calibration circuits in the same job: pick an IBM noise-model simulator (QPU → IBM → Fez) to see the noise, e.g. GHZ-8 parity raw 0.82 → mitigated 0.94 → ideal 1.00. IonQ backends accept one circuit per run, so there only the raw run is made and the console says so. ZNE and the mirror reward cannot run through Qollab's compiler (it cancels folded and mirrored structure, see Limitations), so the full arm is scored offline in the replay tables.

### Locally

```bash
git clone https://github.com/I-ala-Wilson/automitigate && cd automitigate
python3.12 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate   # Windows: py -3.12 -m venv .venv && .venv\Scripts\activate
python -m pip install --upgrade pip
pip install -e ".[sweep,dev]"
pytest -q                                                # 37 tests, ~15 s
python -c "import main; main.main(circuit='ghz8', live=False)"   # the replay the Qollab demo runs (needs matplotlib)
```

On your own circuit and backend: `automitigate.arms.transpile_once` then `apply_arm(qc_t, ARMS[a], twin_rng_seed)` gives the circuits for arm `a` and the target bitstring; `automitigate.score.score_pull` turns their counts into the reward; any learner in `automitigate.learners` does `select()` / `update(arm, reward)`. `scripts/sweep.py` is that loop over a noise model and `main.py` is it over stored rewards.

## Analysis plan and threats to validity

[`docs/analysis_plan.md`](docs/analysis_plan.md) was committed before the first drift reward row existed and since then only its dated typo log has changed. Threats to validity, in full there:

1. Simulation, not hardware: every reward comes from Aer with a noise model rebuilt from calibration data.
2. Markovian, local model: no crosstalk, leakage, non-Markovian or time-correlated noise within a day.
3. The coherent fraction of the two-qubit error is assumed (0 primary, 0.3 sensitivity); under coherent error the mirror construction partially echoes the over-rotation and flatters the non-twirled arms.
4. One device, one chain per circuit size (median-chain sensitivity reported).
5. The mirror proxy is not the user's observable (per-day agreement reported).
6. Synthetic drift on the IonQ-like source.
7. Qollab's IonQ simulator is itself a model of the device.
8. Daily snapshots, last value carried forward: sub-daily recalibrations are not resolved.
9. The dynamic oracle uses seed-mean rewards, an unattainable reference.
10. Qollab's compiler cancels mirror and folded structure on every backend we tested (identity chains of 40–66 gates return P ≈ 1 even with barriers), so ZNE and mirror scoring cannot be executed through Qollab; the IonQ-like constants are therefore published-figure defaults, not fitted, and the spot check is informative only where injected errors survive compilation.

```bash
python scripts/fetch_calibration.py                       # HF phanerozoic/qiskit-calibration-drift, revision pinned in automitigate/noise_model.py

```

`python scripts/run_queue.py --laptop ian --dry-run` lists the exact stage order the two laptops ran. Cost per (day, circuit, 8 arms, 1 seed) cell on a 2024 MacBook Pro, density-matrix method: ghz4 0.3 s, qft3 0.5 s, ghz8 1.4 s, grover3 1.5 s, qaoa6 3.2 s; the 20-seed IBM study is roughly ten laptop-hours, split over two machines. Drift constants for the IonQ-like source are in `data/calib/ab.json`; the Qollab spot-check job IDs in `data/ionq/spot_check.json`; every analysis number in `data/analysis/*.json`.

## Extend it

- **An arm:** add its dict to `ARMS` in `automitigate/arms.py` and the matching branch in `apply_arm`; `shots_per_circuit` keeps the budget equal.
- **A learner:** subclass `Learner` in `automitigate/learners.py` with `select()` / `update(arm, reward)` / `reset()` and add it to `LEARNERS`; the demo form picks it up by name.
- **A circuit:** a function returning a `QuantumCircuit` without measurements in `automitigate/circuits.py`, registered in `CIRCUITS`; add its forward observable to `automitigate/forward.py` if you want the proxy validation.
- **A backend:** anything with `.run(circuits, shots=...)` returning Qiskit-style counts.

## Related work

One line each in [`docs/related_work.md`](docs/related_work.md). In short: CMAB-ZNE (arXiv 2608.06426) is a contextual bandit over ZNE fold sets only; GSC-QEMit (2604.24551) uses simulator knobs as arms; APGEM (2610.01253) needs the ideal expectation value for its reward. None evaluates non-stationary learners under real device drift. The mirror-circuit reward follows Proctor et al. (2008.11294, 2112.09853); the learners follow Agrawal & Goyal 2012, Raj & Kalyani 2017, Trovò et al. 2020 and Cao et al. 2019. arXiv 2507.01195 (raw fidelity stationary within calibration cycles) and 2609.38713 (ZNE hurting on most runs) are the contradiction this replay speaks to: whether the *best mitigation setting* moves, not just the raw fidelity. On this chain it does not; on a worse chain it does.
